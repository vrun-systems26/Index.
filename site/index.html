Index.

Ask for it, and it's in your hand.

Storage assumes you can reach, see, search and remember. Index doesn't. Ask for what you need by voice or from your phone. A 3-axis robot pulls out the exact tray, brings it to one spot within reach, and puts it back when you're done.

Simulation for the web. On the real robot, an ElevenLabs voice agent does the understanding, with the same rules: it only offers what's in stock, it never gives medical advice, and it moves only after you say yes. The robot must reply ACK n for the exact compartment, or the request is rejected.

Selected block

A retrieval, step by step (firmware sequencer)

1 · HomeRetract the rack, then find Z-min and X-min with the limit switches. Skipped if already homed.
2 · MoveX and Z travel together to the taught compartment position.
3 · GrabThe rack extends to the reach angle, the magnet turns on, and after a 400 ms settle the tray is pulled out.
4 · DeliverRise to the delivery height, move across, and set the tray on top. The magnet keeps holding.
5 · ReturnGo in 3 mm higher, push in, magnet off, slide up 10 mm, retract, park, then release the motors.
Build log

From a rail on a board to a working robot

Hardest problems solved

Trays catchingGoing back in, trays hit the opening. Fix: return 3 mm higher than the pickup height.
Trays dragged outAfter the magnet turned off, friction pulled the tray back out. Fix: slide up 10 mm off the washer before retracting.
Switch bounceLimit switches bounced when released. Fix: debouncing plus a re-arm distance.
Battery in minutesThe drivers held full current while idle. Fix: release the motors when idle, and use a wall supply.
Rail on a railBalancing a long Z rail and the Y rack on the X carriage without wobble.
